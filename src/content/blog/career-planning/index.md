A career plan is not one big decision. It is five honest conversations with yourself: who you are, what you enjoy, what the world will pay for, what happens if it goes wrong, and what you will do on Monday morning.

1. Your personality: the Big Five
2. Your interests: Holland codes
3. The overlap: ikigai
4. Plan A, Plan B and Plan Z
5. Fit-gap analysis and tasks

Keep a notebook open. Each step ends with questions worth writing down.

## Step 1 · Who you are: the Big Five

The Big Five is the best-researched model of personality. It describes you on five spectrums, not in boxes: there is no good or bad end, only a better or worse fit with a given job. A free, validated test such as [bigfive-test.com](https://bigfive-test.com/) takes about ten minutes.

<ul class="viz viz-bars" aria-hidden="true">
<li style="--v:.82"><span>Openness</span></li>
<li style="--v:.64"><span>Conscientiousness</span></li>
<li style="--v:.38"><span>Extraversion</span></li>
<li style="--v:.71"><span>Agreeableness</span></li>
<li style="--v:.56"><span>Emotional stability</span></li>
</ul>

- Which situations at work give you energy, and which drain it?
- Where has one of your traits helped you, and where has it got in the way?
- Picture yourself in five years. What kind of work lets your temperament work for you?

## Step 2 · What you enjoy: Holland codes

John Holland sorted work interests into six types: **R**ealistic (hands and tools), **I**nvestigative (ideas and analysis), **A**rtistic (making things), **S**ocial (helping people), **E**nterprising (leading and persuading) and **C**onventional (order and detail). Your code is your top three letters. The free [O\*NET Interest Profiler](https://onetinterestprofiler.org/) finds yours and the jobs that share it.

<div class="viz viz-hex" aria-hidden="true">
<span style="--k:0">R</span><span style="--k:1">I</span><span style="--k:2" class="on">A</span><span style="--k:3" class="on">S</span><span style="--k:4" class="on">E</span><span style="--k:5">C</span>
</div>

- If money were not a concern, how would you spend a working week?
- Which activities make you lose track of time?
- Which three letters sound like you, and which of the matching jobs surprise you?

## Step 3 · Where it meets the market: ikigai

Steps 1 and 2 describe what you love and how you work best. Ikigai, a Japanese idea of "a reason for being", adds two harder questions: what the world needs and what it will pay for. Where all four circles overlap is the work worth aiming at. Three out of four is a warning: passion without income, or a good salary for work that empties you.

<div class="viz viz-ikigai" aria-hidden="true">
<span class="c1">What you love</span><span class="c2">What you are good at</span><span class="c3">What you can be paid for</span><span class="c4">What the world needs</span><b>ikigai</b>
</div>

- Which roles sit where what you can do meets what employers are hiring for?
- What evidence do you have that the market needs it: job ads, salaries, people already doing it?
- Which circle is the weakest for you right now?

## Step 4 · Plan A, Plan B and Plan Z

From Reid Hoffman and Ben Casnocha's *The Start-up of You*. **Plan A** is the direction you are going now, adjusted as you learn. **Plan B** is a pivot next door, using the same skills, that you switch to if A stalls or something better appears. **Plan Z** is the lifeboat: the job, savings or support that catches you if everything fails. Knowing your Plan Z is what makes it safe to take risks with Plan A.

<div class="viz viz-abz" aria-hidden="true">
<p class="lane a"><b>A</b><span>The path you are building</span></p>
<p class="lane b"><b>B</b><span>The pivot next door</span></p>
<p class="lane z"><b>Z</b><span>The lifeboat</span></p>
</div>

- What is your Plan A, in one sentence?
- Which Plan B would use most of the same skills?
- What is your Plan Z, and how many months would it buy you?

## Step 5 · From gaps to tasks

Now make it concrete. List what your Plan A role needs: skills, qualifications, experience and contacts. Score yourself today and the level the role needs, from 1 to 5. Every gap becomes a task with a deadline and a way to tell it is done.

<div class="viz viz-gap" aria-hidden="true">
<p style="--now:.6;--need:.8"><span>Stakeholder management</span></p>
<p style="--now:.2;--need:.6"><span>Data analysis</span></p>
<p style="--now:.4;--need:.8"><span>Network in the field</span></p>
</div>

| Requirement | Today | Needed | Task |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stakeholder management | 3 | 4 | Lead the next supplier review, by March |
| Data analysis | 1 | 3 | Finish an online course and build one report at work, by June |
| Network in the field | 2 | 4 | Two coffee conversations a month with people in the role |

- What is the one task you could start this week?
